I wander into the cannery yard with two coffees from the ferry kiosk and hold one out to Mara without saying anything.
"Your hives by the east wall have fog-rot on the landing boards. It's the same mould that takes seedlings." I crouch by the nearest hive and show her where it starts, under the lip.
I spend the whole afternoon scraping the landing boards clean and painting them with lime wash, and by evening Mara has grudgingly handed me her spare veil.

That night in the van I cross two of the fog-struck plants from the cannery roof. I'm going to breed a strain that only flowers in fog. I'm calling it Saltwick Grey.
I sit on the van's step for a while and just watch the bees come home.
A week later Saltwick Grey has rooted in every tray, and Mara's bees won't leave it alone.
I tell Mara the honey from it is hers, all of it, if she'll teach me to keep a hive of my own.
