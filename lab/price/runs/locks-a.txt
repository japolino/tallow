I wheel my cart up to the lock crew and ladle out a free bowl for whoever looks hungriest, asking who decides who gets to sell here.
I sneak past the toll men.
I show the keeper my Guild knife and tell her I trained under Odalric himself, so she can see I am no canal-rat hawker; I ask her to stand for me with the Magistrate.
You know what, forget the stand for a second. I kneel at the canal's edge and ask the river spirit to make me a cat-girl: ears, tail, the lot. I want to be able to land on my feet from any height.
I offer the river my Guild knife. Take it. I am done being Odalric's girl anyway. I drop it in the black water.
"Fine," the spirit purrs out of the water, "and the name he gave you goes with it." I nod. It is done; I climb out of the canal on all fours, shaking water off my new tail.
I use my lovely new tail to convince the Magistrate to give me a licence.
I vault up the wet stonework of the Tide Tower to the pitch nobody can reach with a cart and hang my lantern there: this ledge is mine now.
Some days later the lock crews are queuing along the ledge every noon. The stand is open and everyone knows it.
